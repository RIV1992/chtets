# Genre decisions

These are editorial guidelines, not QuestBench findings about writing quality. Read only the relevant section. They are conditional choices, not mandatory templates. The current request takes priority over a default structure, length, or profile.

## Email and short messages

A short request may need only the action and necessary context. If the reader must decide something, make the request easy to find. In a dispute or service failure, separate the observation, consequence, and requested action when all three matter. Do not add an unsupported accusation or soften the wording until the request disappears.

In a long thread, repeat only what the recipient needs to answer this message. Add a subject for a new email when useful; a text message or brief reply does not need one. Do not invent recipients, attachments, copied colleagues, amounts, dates, or commitments.

**English:** Match directness to the relationship and task. Do not add corporate excitement or pressure absent from the intent. Distinguish a request, an expectation, and a commitment: “we expect” does not guarantee an event. Preserve currencies, dates, negation, conditions, and actors when translating.

**French:** Translate the communicative purpose, not the original word order. Preserve *tu/vous*, the addressee, and the level of commitment: *nous prévoyons* is not *nous nous engageons*. Use direct, polite requests suited to the situation. Follow a supplied greeting or sign-off preference; no single closing formula is mandatory for every author or message.

**Russian:** Choose the greeting and *ты/вы* according to the relationship. Avoid making familiar correspondence bureaucratic. Prefer the actual action to cumbersome administrative phrasing while keeping necessary terms and identifiers. Preserve the author's urgency; the word “urgent” cannot replace a necessary response time.

Give the finished message first. Explain only a significant change of meaning or an important unresolved gap. Drafting is not authorization to send.

## Interviews and expert comments

Answer the actual question and find a position the author can defend. Make the opening substantive; an arresting line helps only if it answers the question. Develop one thought through its reason or consequence rather than stringing together independent aphorisms.

A personal pronoun does not create a personal voice. Use the author's real observations and decision criteria. Distinguish “I think,” “I do,” “our practice is,” and “we proved.” Do not turn an aspiration into an existing process, advice into company history, or an opinion into a research result.

If a personal episode is needed, ask what happened and what the author chose. A few focused questions are enough: “What happened last time?”, “What did you do?”, or “What made you reconsider?” Do not ask a questionnaire when the material already supports an answer.

A quotation should make sense on its own without losing a condition that changes its meaning. A headline may highlight a specific recommendation; do not present it as a verbatim quotation without a basis. Keep a general answer general unless a company example serves the question.

**Records and recollections require a separate mode.** Do not rewrite a verbatim transcript or testimony merely to make it smoother. Keep factual concerns outside the speaker's words. When an adaptation is requested, preserve the original and distinguish the adaptation from the source.

## Public and social posts

Identify the occasion: observation, news, position, result, or invitation. Do not invent a dramatic personal turning point when none is supplied. Make the opening promise what the post actually delivers.

Choose a connected line of thought: what was noticed, what it means, and why the audience might care. This is one possible structure, not three mandatory blocks. A short announcement may need a single paragraph.

Prefer a supported detail to “unique,” “revolutionary,” or “incredible.” Group connected thoughts rather than putting every sentence on its own line. Questions, calls to action, hashtags, and emojis depend on purpose and voice; do not solicit comments just to manufacture engagement.

Names and claimed results must match the source material. For different platforms, change the length and entry point while keeping the same factual basis.

## Explanations, analysis, and technical reports

**Explanation:** Identify the step the reader does not understand and make it visible through one sufficient example. Explain a term when it first becomes necessary; do not begin with a full glossary. Use an analogy when it reveals the mechanism, and state its limits when they matter. Simplify the route to understanding while preserving conditions required for the conclusion to remain correct. Use a diagram when relationships or order are easier to see than to describe.

**Analysis:** Separate data, interpretation, alternative explanation, and recommendation. Show support where the conclusion depends on it without turning each paragraph into a verification log. Compare alternatives using relevant criteria without deciding the winner in advance. Treat uncertainty and scope as part of the conclusion. Do not present a custom scoring scheme as an established scientific method.

**Scientific or technical report:** Establish the purpose and required form from the actual brief and governing documents. Persuasive prose cannot replace a reproducible account or supporting records. Separate planned work, completed work, observed results, and interpretation. Preserve experimental conditions, definitions, units, notation, limitations, and evidence links. Do not fill gaps with expected results or treat a claim of novelty as an established finding. When standards matter, verify applicability and edition rather than choosing by a similar title. Follow the required sections and terminology instead of imposing a journalistic structure.

## Interface copy

Write for the actual system state: awaiting an action, processing, completed, or failed. Do not report sending, saving, or success until the corresponding event is confirmed.

A button label should predict the next action. Include the object or irreversibility when relevant. An error message should identify an available next step when it is known. Do not promise retries, recovery, or data preservation without matching product behavior.

Use consistent names for the same entity. Brevity must not erase distinct states. Check localized strings in screen context, including pronouns and plural forms. Include a technical cause when it helps the user decide or solve the problem.

## Creative writing

Identify the intended scene, voice, tension, image, or formal experiment. Do not search for a business thesis by habit. Rhythm, repetition, silence, long sentences, metaphor, and contradiction can serve the work; judge them by their effect rather than a ban list.

Preserve viewpoint, temporal logic, and deliberate ambiguity while removing accidental confusion. Invention is allowed within the declared creative task, but it does not become the author's biography. Do not attribute an invented memory or action to a real person as factual personal testimony.

Lead with the finished text. Explain the design and editorial choices only when asked or when an intervention changes the source's meaning.
