# Editorial review

Use this reference for complex work and requested audits. The two passes in SKILL.md are enough for a short edit. This is an editorial rubric, not a scientific quality scale.

## Defects that block completion

Do not consider a text ready while it has a material defect such as:

- a missing main request or required condition;
- an unsupported fact, personal episode, number, commitment, or quotation;
- a changed amount, date, actor, degree of certainty, negation, or scope;
- a citation that contradicts or fails to support its claim;
- a conclusion based on false causation, a changed definition, or a faulty answer key;
- a violation of explicit limits on rewriting, language, authorship, length, or format.

Polished prose or a high aggregate score cannot compensate for these errors.

## Check six qualities

| Quality | Observable check | What to repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task completion | The reader gets the required point or action | Missing request, wrong scope |
| Accuracy | Claims match their grounds and limits | Added facts, stronger certainty |
| Coherence | The reader can see why one sentence follows another | Topic jumps, missing premises |
| Specificity | It is clear who does what and what changes | Empty abstractions, slogans |
| Voice and fit | The wording suits this author, reader, and occasion | Generic corporate tone, unnatural translation |
| Economy | Further cuts would remove a useful function or meaning | Repetition, cumbersome syntax, needless openings |

Discuss actual passages. A rating without an explanation does not repair anything. If scores are requested, explain the criteria and their subjective nature rather than calling them objective measures of writing ability.

## Read from the audience's position

What will the reader know after the first paragraph? Where must they guess a connection? Which fact would they want to check? Does the headline overpromise? Is the next action clear when the genre calls for one? Do not require a call to action in work that does not need it.

When shortening, compare the original and final text specifically for required meanings. Check for a lost caveat, condition, deadline, reason, or important counterargument. Compress syntax rather than accuracy.

## Evaluating a new version of the skill

Use this section only when asked to develop or evaluate Chtets, not during every edit.

1. Select realistic task types and prepare fresh source material. Do not use the reference examples and their known answers as the test.
2. Define required conditions and unacceptable errors before generating outputs. Allow multiple legitimate stylistic solutions.
3. Compare ordinary prompting and the skill on identical material under comparable conditions. Do not give either writer the expected answer.
4. Hide the conditions from the reviewing editor and vary presentation order where possible. Preserve the original outputs.
5. Record constraint violations separately from stylistic preference. Check whether fluency came at the cost of meaning.
6. Change a rule only in response to an observed defect, then test the relevant issue on fresh material.

A small trial can illustrate behavior on those tasks; it cannot establish a general improvement. Model self-review does not replace the author's judgment. Report ties and mixed findings as such. An evaluation of an earlier version does not validate this public version or every translation.

When asked to retain feedback, record the request, weak passage, reason, accepted repair, and scope. Do not turn each comment into a permanent prohibition or silently update the skill during ordinary writing work.
