# Practice pairs

All situations below are invented for teaching. They are not claims about a real author or organization. Each fact set permits specificity only within its own example.

### 1. A missing connection

**Given:** Test invitations land in spam; the launch depends on their delivery.

**Before:** “Emails are landing in spam. I suggest postponing the launch.”

**After:** “Test emails are landing in spam. Until delivery is working, recipients may miss their invitation. I suggest postponing the launch.”

The revision connects the observation to the proposal while keeping the risk a possibility.

### 2. An unestablished cause

**Given:** Support requests increased after an update; the cause is unknown.

**Before:** “The update caused an increase in support requests.”

**After:** “Support requests increased after the update. We do not yet know whether the update contributed to the increase.”

The revision preserves the observation and the limit of current knowledge.

### 3. Impersonal vagueness

**Given:** The author will send a corrected invoice today.

**Before:** “The documentation issue will be resolved promptly.”

**After:** “I'll send the corrected invoice today.”

The action, actor, and date come from the supplied facts.

### 4. Dangerous compression

**Given:** Only a plan for partial payment by Friday has been confirmed.

**Before:** “We are planning to make a partial repayment of the outstanding debt by Friday.”

**After:** “We plan to repay part of the debt by Friday.”

“We will repay the debt on Friday” is shorter but changes the amount, certainty, and timing.

### 5. Rhythm and connection

**Given:** The team cannot check the settings without access.

**Before:** “We need access. Without access, we cannot check the settings. Please give us access.”

**After:** “Please grant us access so we can check the settings.”

Combining the sentences preserves the reason for the request and removes repetition.

### 6. Persuasion without an invented biography

**Given:** The author values reporting an error before finding a solution.

**Before:** “Everyone on our team must report an error within five minutes.”

**After:** “I want people to flag an error immediately, even when they do not have a solution yet.”

The revision states the author's position and removes an invented rule and metric.
