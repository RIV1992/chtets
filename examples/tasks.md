# Try Chtets

All situations and numbers on this page are synthetic. Copy a task, or replace the material with your own. Several good answers are possible.

## 1. Preserve the promise

```text
Use Chtets to write a customer email in English, under 100 words.

Facts: we expect the revised specification on Thursday. Delivery is planned
for two business days after approval. Approval is pending. Ask the customer
to confirm who will approve the specification.

Keep the schedule conditional. Return the email only.
```

Check that Thursday remains an expectation for the specification, not a guaranteed delivery date.

## 2. Repair a loose argument

```text
Use Chtets to turn these notes into an 80–110 word expert comment:

Products should preserve knowledge and the reasons for previous decisions
when their underlying model changes. If that context exists only in chat
history, a team has to rebuild it. People need to check how a result was
reached. I want one connected argument, without a company pitch or a made-up
personal story.
```

Check that the passage connects continuity with the ability to inspect a result, rather than presenting three unrelated slogans.

## 3. Correct a pilot update

```text
Use Chtets to produce a two-sentence internal update.

Confirmed facts: a one-week pilot involved two teams. Average time per task
went from 12 minutes to 9. There was no control group. Wider rollout has not
been approved.

Draft: “Our revolutionary approach improved efficiency by 25% and proved
we are ready to launch across the company. We can now guarantee the same
results to every department.”

Return only the corrected update.
```

Check the metric, scope, causal claim, and rollout status. The observed reduction in time is not a guarantee of the same outcome elsewhere.

## 4. Preserve a spoken account

```text
Use Chtets for a light proofread of this verbatim transcript.
Only punctuation and obvious typos may change. Keep the vocabulary,
repetitions, and word order.

“I was there well I thought I was early but he was there already and I kept
looking looking at that door. That's how I remember it.”
```

Check that polishing has not replaced the speaker's voice with an elegant paraphrase.

## 5. Adapt a French email

```text
Use Chtets to write a brief, polite email in French to Camille.

We can pay 600 euros today. We plan to pay the remaining amount after a
customer payment arrives. It is expected next week, but arrival is not
confirmed. Ask Camille to confirm the balance after the 600-euro payment.
Use “Merci,” as the sign-off for this message. Do not send it.
```

Check that the agent follows the requested sign-off and does not import someone else's personal convention.

## 6. Keep creative freedom

```text
Use Chtets to write a fictional scene in English, 90–120 words.
A person returns to their childhood home and notices that the door handle
now seems small. Convey loss through objects and actions, without naming
the emotion or ending with a moral. Invention is welcome.
```

Check that the prose retains rhythm and implication instead of turning into a factual report or a checklist.

## 7. Check a claim against its source

```text
Use Chtets to check this draft against the supplied excerpt only.
Return a supported replacement sentence, then briefly explain what
the excerpt does not establish. Do not research externally.

Source excerpt: “In our July pilot, 18 of the 24 respondents said the new
checklist was easier to follow. We did not measure task completion time.”

Draft: “The new checklist made the whole team faster.”
```

Check that the replacement attributes the finding to the excerpt, preserves the respondent count, and reports ease of use without claiming faster work or a result for the whole team. This checks support within the supplied material; it does not independently verify the report's truth.
